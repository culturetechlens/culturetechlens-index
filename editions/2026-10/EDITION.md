## The question

"What can an ordinary object remember that the history books forgot?" — the
question behind LIFE-001, *How We Lived: An Encyclopedia of Black Everyday Life*.
This edition answers it with data: the ten everyday artifacts most resonant in
the combined record of public attention and CTL's own verified research.

Rankings describe the data. They do not crown winners.

## The CTL 10 — Artifacts, October 2026

**1. Cadillac (as cultural object) — 78.8**
The month's highest public attention (33,922 lookups) meets deep CTL
documentation: the car as a documented vessel of Black aspiration and mobility.

**2. Funeral-home advertising church fan — 67.8**
Strong on both signals. The everyday sacred object — part worship aid, part
community bulletin board — that the archive remembers.

**3. Yearbook (1955 Allen University "Yellow Jacket") — 66.5**
The month's highest momentum: institutional Black memory gaining attention.

**4. Mail-order catalog (Sears / Montgomery Ward) — 65.4**
Balanced across all signals: the catalog as documented infrastructure of Black
consumer life, also rising in public attention.

**5. Transistor radio (Regency TR-1) — 65.3**
Balanced across all three signals: the pocket soundtrack of a generation.

**6. Murray's Superior Hair Dressing Pomade (tin) — 62.4**
Steady across the board: the shelf object with a century of documented presence.

**7. Hot comb / pressing comb — 57.1**
Deep CTL evidence but modest public attention (958 lookups) — the record holds
more than the public currently seeks.

**8. 45-rpm single (Chess 1604, "Maybellene") — 47.5**
Moderate public attention, thinner CTL evidence (mostly provisional claims) —
a documented gap in our own corpus, flagged openly.

**9. Afro pick (fist-handle hair pick) — 46.2**
The edition's signature finding: the deepest CTL documentation of all ten
subjects, yet the lowest public attention (106 lookups). The most documented
object is the least looked up.

**10. The Black family photograph album — 22.6**
The thinnest evidence in the set — no Object Biography yet. The Index
surfacing its own research gap: an invitation, not a verdict.

## How it was scored

- **Public attention (40%):** trailing-30-day Wikipedia pageviews per subject's closest article, log-normalized across the set. (Church fan → Hand fan; pomade tin → Pomade; album → Photo album.)
- **Evidence depth (40%):** from CTL-DR-LIFE-001, the edition's source research package — verified and provisional claim counts, relationship counts, and full Object Biography bonus, normalized across the set.
- **Momentum (20%):** pageview trend, trailing 30 days vs. prior 30 days; 50 = flat.
- Computed by `ctl-index.py`; no hand-tuned scores. Full per-signal data: `index-scores.csv`.

## Limitations

- Wikipedia pageviews skew toward subjects with English Wikipedia articles and internet-era attention.
- CTL's corpus reflects what CTL has researched so far; under-documented subjects score lower on evidence depth by construction.
- The Index reflects resonance in the record, not community sentiment on the ground.
- Ranks move month to month; movement is signal, not verdict.
